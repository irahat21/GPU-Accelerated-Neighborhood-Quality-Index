About NYC Quality Index

NYC Quality Index is an interactive quality-of-life mapping platform built to help users compare New York City areas using public data. The current prototype combines four core categories, normalizes them onto a shared 0-100 scale, and lets users explore results across multiple NYC geographies.

Our Mission

Our goal is to turn scattered public datasets into a single, readable system for comparing neighborhood conditions. Rather than making users search through multiple city data portals, NYC Quality Index presents environmental, educational, and public-safety indicators in one place.

What The App Measures

The current prototype focuses on four categories:

  • Air Quality: pollution burden using PM2.5, NO2, and O3 measurements
  • Drinking Water: sampled water quality indicators from public monitoring sites
  • Education: school-level quality inputs aggregated into area-level scores
  • Public Safety: NYPD complaint burden using severity-weighted incident totals

Current Methodology

NYC Quality Index is built at the community-district level first. Air and public safety were already available in district-ready form, while water and education were spatially aggregated into community districts and then merged into one combined score table.

Each metric is converted to a normalized 0-100 quality scale so different data types can be compared side by side. Higher values indicate better conditions for the displayed metric.

Overall Score Calculation

Overall Score = (Air + Water + Education + Public Safety) / 4

The overall score is an equal average of the four normalized subscores. This is the official scoring method used throughout the current NYC Quality Index platform and across every geography view shown in the application.

Air Quality

Air quality is based on monitored pollutant burden using PM2.5, NO2, and O3 values. Lower raw pollution burden corresponds to a higher normalized quality score.

  • PM2.5 is used as a fine-particle pollution indicator
  • NO2 reflects traffic and combustion-related pollution burden
  • O3 captures ozone-related air quality conditions
  • The displayed score is normalized to a 0-100 scale
  • Higher values indicate better air quality
  • Typical interpretation: 90-100 excellent, 70-89 good, 50-69 moderate, 30-49 below average, 0-29 needs attention

Drinking Water

Drinking water quality starts from site-level monitoring records and is spatially assigned into community districts before being rolled up into the app's geographic views.

  • Residual free chlorine
  • Turbidity
  • Fluoride
  • Coliform-related indicators where available
  • Area-level rollups preserve sample-site count context
  • The displayed score is normalized to a 0-100 scale
  • Higher values indicate better drinking water quality
  • Typical interpretation: 90-100 excellent, 70-89 good, 50-69 moderate, 30-49 below average, 0-29 needs attention

Education Quality

Education quality starts from school-level results and is aggregated into community-district scores before being reused across the map's larger or alternate geography views.

  • School-level education quality inputs
  • Spatial assignment of schools to districts
  • Average area-level education scoring
  • School counts remain visible in the detail views
  • The displayed score is normalized to a 0-100 scale
  • Higher values indicate better education quality
  • Typical interpretation: 90-100 excellent, 70-89 good, 50-69 moderate, 30-49 below average, 0-29 needs attention

Public Safety Score

Public safety is derived from NYPD complaint data using a severity-weighted incident framework.

  • Complaint severity weighting by incident type
  • Weighted incident burden totals
  • Area-level incident counts
  • Normalization to a 0-100 quality score
  • Higher values indicate better public safety conditions
  • Typical interpretation: 90-100 excellent, 70-89 good, 50-69 moderate, 30-49 below average, 0-29 needs attention

Higher scores indicate fewer or less severe complaint burdens and therefore better public safety conditions.

Geography Views

The app now supports multiple ways to view the same prototype score system:

  • Community District: the core geography where the prototype scores are built directly
  • Borough: borough-level rollups from the community-district scores
  • ZIP Code: area-weighted rollups onto modified ZIP code tabulation areas
  • Neighborhood: area-weighted rollups onto NYC Neighborhood Tabulation Areas (NTAs)

This lets users compare the same quality framework at different spatial resolutions depending on whether they want a planning-district view, a borough overview, a ZIP-level view, or an official neighborhood-style view.

How To Read The Scores

Every map view uses the same basic interpretation: higher normalized values indicate better relative conditions within the current prototype. The interface also exposes supporting context such as rank, sample-site count, school count, incident count, and weighted severity burden where relevant.

Data Sources

All data is sourced from official New York City agencies and public datasets:

  • Air Quality: NYC Open Data air quality measurements
  • Water Safety: NYC Department of Environmental Protection (DEP)
  • Education: NYC Department of Education Open Data
  • Public Safety: NYPD Complaint Data (NYC Open Data)
